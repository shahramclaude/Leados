## Context

Every prior session in this register family dealt with what the entity **says outward**: ADR-0234 closes the promise the entity gave a regulator, a board or a lender. Nothing in the workspace dealt with the other half of the relationship — the day an authority **asks the entity for records** and sets a date for them.

That gap is not a paperwork gap. In practice it is where examination findings are actually manufactured:

1. **The date is somebody else's.** A supervisor's information request, a production notice, a subpoena and a privacy query each carry a reply day that the entity does not own. Every existing register in the workspace tracks a day the entity *declared*. A deadline nobody can see, on a clock nobody can look up in this app, is a deadline that quietly passes.
2. **The answer is assembled by several hands and certified by none of them.** One demand lands on operations, legal, finance and IT at once. The failure mode is not that a line misses its day; it is that the *bundle* goes out labelled "complete" because the person who compiled it said so — while the line that withheld material under privilege was never tested by anybody who did not withhold it.
3. **A privilege claim is the most self-serving sentence in the record.** "214 items withheld — legally privileged" is written by the party that benefits from it. In real examination practice that claim is the single most common source of a follow-up demand, an adverse inference, or a waived privilege. No control in this workspace existed to force an independent ruling on it.
4. **Sent is not accepted.** A production goes out through a portal, and the requester either says nothing, or says it did not cover the ask. The first is recorded nowhere, so it reads as done; the second is recorded in a mailbox, so it never reaches the register that will be read in a year.

The structural defect this workspace had, and this ADR removes, is the **multi-line production gap** by construction: a single-owner record model cannot express "four lines, four producers, one answer, one reviewer who produced nothing." ADR-0234 built the line machinery for the outward direction; this is the inbound mirror, and it inherits the joint apex, the line scope and the identity-free posture deliberately.

## Decision

**Four separate facts, one release, and a day that never decides.** The register records: (1) **the demand** — requester, closed-list requester kind, closed-list instrument, declared scope, receipt day, and the **declared reply clock in whole days**; (2) **the lines** — one to four, each with its own subject, its own day and its own *named* producer; (3) **the certification and the claim** — per line, what was searched, the period and systems searched, the item count, a required boolean `complete`, and separately a privilege claim that must carry a count above zero and a basis; (4) **the review** — per line, from a seat outside the parties, with a **required** ruling on the privilege claim whenever one stands. Only then may (5) **the release** be recorded, naming the transmission and an item count that cannot exceed the sum the certified productions add up to; and the requester's **verdict** is a sixth, separate fact (`received-complete | no-response | insufficient`).

**Independence is defined by conflict, not by rank.** `rqrReviewEligibility` in `rqRegistry.js` is the ONE test the kernel, the reader, the badge, the dialogs, the action and the rule all call, in this order: (a) **non-participation** — whoever recorded the demand, produces any line, or holds any act on the ledger is a party and cannot review; (b) **graph connection** — the reviewer must sit in the producers' connected reporting graph; (c) **standing, not rank** — the reviewer must hold rank at or above the *least senior* producer, so an IC never grades a manager's production. A `fullAccess` operator view cannot review: a completeness certificate is a first-person act with a name on it. Producers and reviewers are the *only* rank-1+ rights in the module; recording, staffing, releasing and answering are rank 3+, and the seat right and the action's own floor are the same number on purpose (a dead gate is a lie about a control).

**Nothing is invented, least of all a deadline.** There is no jurisdiction table, no default 30-day window, no inferred producer and no assumed scope. A stored row whose `replyClockDays` is missing or fractional is dropped by the loader and **counted**, never repaired to a number. A `complete` answer is required, not defaulted. A privilege claim with no count is refused. `0` items produced is legal and means "searched, found nothing". A producer is *named*, and naming them immediately disqualifies them from reviewing.

**The reply day belongs to the requester.** The effective day is the later of the recorded day, an extension **and** a restated clock, and both are bounded: an extension is refused outright on an instrument whose date is not the entity's to move (`rqrInstrumentIsCompelled` — subpoena, court order, statutory notice), refused on every other instrument without the requester's own recorded agreement, refused unless it moves forward, refused beyond the declared horizon, and refused once the day has passed. The only path for a deadline that moved *without* the entity asking is `restate-clock`, which requires a citation to the requester's own letter and a restated day that agrees with `receivedOn + their clock` — a number matching neither is refused (`clockNotRestated`).

**A passed day is printed, never converted.** `reply-lapsed` is a derived state with `lapsed = true` and it is not discharged by moving a date, by a review, or by time. `released-answered` is the only state asserting the requester received it and said so; `insufficient` outranks it, so a production the requester rejected never reads as an answer. `rates.releasedInsideWindow` counts only requests whose day has passed, and a lapsed request sits in the denominator and never the numerator.

**Aggregate, attribute, or refuse — and know which you are doing.** The panel names producers, reviewers and requesters, because a register where nobody is accountable for a line is decoration. The **export** names nobody: CSV #178 is counts and blanks only, a requester-kind count below the privacy floor of three exports blank with its reason, and every ask `RULE-RQR-01` files is deliberately opaque — no requester, instrument, scope, line or date, with the signature carried only in the typed `issues-obligation` link. The register never rates a person or a function from backlog or age; `no-authority` and `no-independent-reviewer` are printed as facts about the graph, and nobody is promoted to fill them.

## Consequences

**Gained.** The workspace can now answer, from one register, the question an examiner actually asks first — "show me what you were told to produce, what you produced, who checked it, and when you sent it" — and it can refuse to answer in the way that gets entities in trouble: a bundle labelled complete over an untested privilege claim is now unrepresentable, because the release is gated on the review and the review is gated on non-participation.

**Deliberate omissions, stated plainly.** (1) No demo seeding: an empty register reads honestly as empty, and seeding a fake demand with a fake statutory clock would put a fabricated deadline on the record — the exact fact this module refuses to invent (same call as ADR-0233/0234). (2) No `insights.js` bucket and no `cases.js` entity type: the register's graph nodes are persons and delegations, matching the family precedent; a second node type would let a demand be linked as if it were a case. (3) No settings card: the one convention, `informationRequestAutoPropagate`, is changed through the pause-permit door that governs every other rule, not through a second, ungoverned path. (4) No document store, no OCR, no privileged-content handling — this register records that material was searched, claimed, reviewed and released; it never holds the material, which is why the seat class ceiling is `reportOnly` at every altitude. (5) No jurisdiction or statutory-window table by design — see §Decision.

**Costs.** Nine write commands and seven act kinds is the most acting this family has carried; the price is that every one of them has a refusal with a reason, and the panel's "Refused acts" tab is where an operator learns why a button is absent. The independence test is also the strictest yet: on a single-line demand recorded by the producer's own manager, the register may legitimately find **no** eligible reviewer, print `no-independent-reviewer`, and refuse to release — an organisation that wants to answer its regulators needs a second leadership branch, and the register says so instead of waiving.

## Benchmark

**[BENCHMARK-UNVERIFIED]** — judged against published product descriptions, not a side-by-side run this session. The category leaders for this exact workflow are **Logix Experts Regulatory Management** (regulator requests, RFIs and examination tasks with owners, deadlines and escalation), **Thomson Reuters Navigation Catalyst / Regulatory Intelligence** (obligation-to-request linkage), **SAI360 Regulatory & Examination Management**, and, for the production half, the litigation stack: **Relativity**, **Everlaw** and **Custodian**, where a production set, a privilege log and a reviewer who is not the document's custodian are ordinary features.

Against that bar the register **matches** on four checkable dimensions: a dated inbound demand with an owner and a real deadline; production split into lines with named producers; a privilege log that requires a count and a basis; and a release event distinct from the work. It **exceeds** the GRC products on two points, because they are workflow tools rather than ledgers: the reviewer-independence rule is *structural* (the party set is computed from the ledger, not configured by an administrator who can leave it off), and the day is *never* the decider — no escalation converts a lapsed request into an answered one, and no default window exists to be quietly wrong. It **exceeds** the e-discovery stack on the mirror point: those tools assume the reviewer's independence and never model the *organisation's* obligation to the requester, so they hold no reply day, no requester verdict and no privilege *ruling* — only a claim.

It **does not match, and does not claim to**: collection processing, TAR/coding at volume, OCR, bates numbering, native-file handling, privilege-log export to a court format, jurisdiction-specific statutory calendars, and any regulator portal integration. Those are the tools' actual product, and pretending otherwise would be the kind of invented capability NORTH_STAR forbids. **Unverified:** no vendor environment was run; the comparison rests on published descriptions and practitioner literature.
