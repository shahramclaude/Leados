## Context

Permit-to-work is the oldest control in industrial safety and the one every site still runs on paper pinned to a board. UK HSE **HSG250** defines the permit as a formal written system of work, issued by an authorised person, for a defined task over a defined validity — and is explicit that **the permit does not by itself make a plant safe**. OSHA **29 CFR 1910.147** (control of hazardous energy) and **1910.146** (permit-required confined spaces) require the isolation to be *applied* and the zero-energy state to be *verified at the point of work* before work starts. **NFPA 51B** requires a named fire watch for hot work. Every one of those is a record requirement, and every one of them fails in practice in the same six ways:

1. **The permit is authorised and the isolation is assumed.** Somebody racked a breaker; nobody proved the terminals dead. The permit says "isolated"; the record cannot say who checked, when, or against what window.
2. **The proof is late and called on time.** A verification recorded an hour after the declared window is printed as a verification. The window is the control; a proof outside it proves nothing about the world at the moment work started.
3. **The isolation is released and nobody above notices.** A crew finishes early, restores the line, and goes home. The line is now live beside a second permit that nobody has compared it with.
4. **Two permits each look fine.** Hot work in a tank room and a line break in the same tank room are individually authorised, individually safe, and together the reason the tank room burned. This is the entire discipline of **SIMOPS** — simultaneous operations — and it cannot be done from a filing cabinet.
5. **The gas test is a tick box.** A confined-space entry with an oxygen reading and no hydrogen-sulphide reading, against a permit that never declared what to test for, is not a test.
6. **The closure hides the plant.** A permit closed while a lock is still on leaves a line isolated with nobody aware of it, and the paperwork has already stopped looking.

The vendor bar is set by the e-PTW and LOTO category: pre-Sapien, Intelex, EHS Insight, KPA, PermitiVision, VelocityEHS and Brady eLockout **[BENCHMARK-VERIFIED against fetched vendor descriptions, 2026-11-17]**. Their common core is exactly this register: typed permit kinds, an isolation certificate with apply / verify / remove, SIMOPS conflict detection, closure verification, and an audit trail. **LeadOS already holds the roster, the reporting graph and the delegation spine that none of them have**, so this is a category where the workspace can match the specialism and exceed it on routing, on arithmetic and on what it refuses to claim.

## Decision

Build the register as one reader over seven append-only ledgers, with the states **derived on every read** and **never stored**.

**1. The permit declares, the reader cannot invent.** A permit names its work reference, its area, its work kind, its authority kind, its holder, its issuer, a **written basis reference**, a whole-minute validity, a start instant, an optional declared first entry (`flightFromAt`), a declared fire watch where the work kind needs one, a crew, and — where the work kind is inherently energetic — an **energy control**. Everything else in the system is derived from those declarations. A permit with a missing basis, a fractional or zero validity, an unreadable work kind, an undeclared or duplicated atmosphere limit, or a confined-space entry with no test plan is **dropped with a counted warning and never repaired**.

**2. Two things are kept and flagged rather than erased.** A **self-issued** permit (holder = issuer) and a **hot-work permit with no named fire watch** are both loader-valid rows that the reader prints as what they are: the loader's job is to read the record, and a control that deletes a breach destroys the evidence the control exists to produce. The door refuses to *create* either.

**3. Energy control is declared, then forced where the kind is inherently energetic.** Each work kind carries `inherentlyEnergy` (true for hot work, cold work, confined-space entry, electrical isolation, line breaking and excavation; false for work at height and general). The permit-level `energyControl` is an issuer declaration; for an inherently energetic kind the door **forces** it true and refuses a `energy-control-forced` waiver, and the loader stamps `energyControlForced` on the row. A live energy-control permit with **zero** isolations on the record reads `live-unverified` with `no-isolation-declared` — never `live`, because on the record there is nothing to show the plant is dead.

**4. Nine states, nine different facts, every one printed with the flags that fired under it.** `live`, `live-unverified`, `live-atmosphere`, `live-atmosphere-stale`, `suspended`, `not-yet-valid`, `expired-open`, `closed-complete`, `closed-isolation-owed`. A suspension outranks the unproved isolation it still carries — and the flag is still printed underneath, because a headline is not a summary. A resumption does **not** restore the pre-suspension record: it re-derives from the same ledgers and the isolation is still unproved.

**5. The arithmetic is exact whole minutes, floored, and refuses rather than guesses.** Minutes to the declared end, minutes past it, minutes since a reading, minutes of overlap between two validity windows, the atmosphere verdict, and the isolation proof window are all computed by pure kernels with the clock injected. A fractional validity has no end instant and is not read as one. Thirty-nine seconds is not a minute.

**6. Conflicts are derived on every read and never stored.** Thirteen closed conflict kinds, each computed from the permit, isolation, reading and roster ledgers: the headline **`hot-work-over-isolation-release`** (an isolation released inside a live hot-work permit's validity in the same area), the honest overflow **`entry-during-hot-work`** when a second live permit shares the area with hot work and **no** release is recorded — which names the overlap and states plainly that no release is recorded rather than inventing one — plus `two-hot-work-same-area`, `atmosphere-out-of-limit`, `atmosphere-stale`, `permit-lapsed-open`, `isolation-verification-overdue`, `closure-isolation-owed`, `isolation-double-claimed`, `holder-double-booked`, `orphan-holder`, `hot-work-without-fire-watch`, `isolation-released-under-live-permit`. **The one thing the reader will not do is require both permits to cite the same isolation before it will name the danger**: a second live permit in the same area with an ignition source is itself the evidence.

**7. The ask goes above the holder, never to them.** `RULE-PTW-01` carries five conditions — `live-unverified`, `live-atmosphere`, `expired-open`, `simultaneous-operations`, `isolation-outstanding` — and files **one ask per obligation**, signed `ptw1:<kind>:<permitId>` (or `ptw1:simultaneous-operations:<A>+<B>`, sorted so the two crews are told once rather than twice). The addressee is the **joint apex above every line holding the holder(s)**, computed by the matrix engine: the line that authorises the work is routinely not the line the holder sits on, and the seat answerable for both is the one above both. It **never** goes to the holder — they are standing next to the plant, and asking somebody to chase their own isolation is the exact failure the rule forbids. Where nobody can be asked, the rule writes **one identity-free `ptwMarkers` observation** per signature instead of asking an invented seat.

**8. The rule is disciplined about its own noise.** Idempotent per (signature, addressee); self-closing when the condition clears, with a `RULE-PTW-01` completion note; it does **not** re-file an ask a human already closed or returned, because a human answer is an answer; capped at **four new asks per commit** with the remainder **counted as deferred** rather than silently dropped; and pause-governed on every path, including the explicit scan — because a permit lapses and a reading goes stale on the calendar, with no user action at all. Its ask text names **no person, area, work reference, lock, instrument or reading**; the opaque signature lives in the typed `issues-obligation` link.

**9. Closing with an isolation outstanding is recorded, never blocked.** The door accepts the closure, returns `outstandingIsolationN`, and the reader prints the permit as `closed-isolation-owed` with a conflict. Refusing the closure would push the event off the record entirely, which is the opposite of what a safety register is for. The door does refuse a **second** closure, and refuses a closure on a permit that is **suspended and not resumed** — the record disagreeing with itself.

**10. The proof window is the permit's own, and the hand that applied is not the hand that proves.** `permitToWorkVerifyIsolation` refuses a proof outside the declared window with the exact window in the message, and refuses a self-verification where a second competent person exists. **A failing gas reading is accepted** — a reading is a fact, and a door that refuses to record a bad reading is the door that makes the record lie.

**11. The export is aggregate, identity-free and honest about what it withholds.** CSV #187 prints one summary row, one row per **state** and one row per **conflict kind** — the kinds are *named*, because a safety export that smoothed them over would be the one thing this category must never do. No person, area, work reference, isolation point, lock, instrument, reading or instant crosses the boundary. A per-state or per-conflict count below the declared floor of 3 exports **BLANK with its reason**, never `0`.

**12. One defect found and fixed in build.** `saved()` in the actions layer overwrote a caller's own warning with `store.persistError || null`, so the closure's "N isolation(s) were never removed and confirmed" message was **silently dropped on every clean persist** — the exact class of silent refusal the doctrine forbids. Caught by the focused suite; fixed by merging caller warning and persist error instead of replacing one with the other.

## Consequences

- The workspace can now answer, from its own record: which work is authorised, on which plant, for how many more whole minutes, on what proof of zero energy and recorded when; which isolations were applied, proved inside their window or missed it, and whether their removal was confirmed; which gas tests were declared, which were never recorded, and which reading has failed the permit's own limit by how much; who holds which permit across which lines; and exactly which simultaneous-operations conflicts exist, each with its arithmetic and the two permits it joins.
- The demo record exercises the whole vocabulary against the injected clock, so nothing in the demo is a state that was only true on the day it was written: a **tank-room hot work** permit live with a fire watch; a **pump-house electrical** permit on an isolation whose 30-minute window was never proved; a **tank-room line break** whose isolation was released two hours ago and confirmed — the headline conflict with the hot work beside it; a **sump entry** live with a passing oxygen reading and a **failing H₂S reading of 12 ppm against a declared limit of 5**; a **warehouse** permit lapsed three hours ago and never closed; a **compressor** permit cleanly closed with both isolations restored; and a **cooling-tower** permit closed with one lock still outstanding.
- **Refusals are the product.** Named refusals include `permit-not-found`, `closure-outcome-invalid`, `closure-note-missing`, `already-closed`, `suspended-cannot-close`, `close-before-permit`, `instant-future`, `energy-control-forced`, `isolation-before-…` (deliberately removed — see below), `verify-outside-window`, `self-verification`, `holder-double-booked`, `fire-watch-missing`, `atmosphere-plan-missing`, `unreadable`.
- **Pre-validity isolations and readings are deliberately legal.** The real industrial sequence is to isolate the plant and test the atmosphere **before** authorising work; the first schema attempt refused an isolation applied before the permit's validity start and was wrong. That refusal was removed from both the loader and the door and must not be reintroduced.
- **Honest limitations, carried in the product:** the register cannot inspect a plant and never asserts that anything is safe or unsafe as a matter of fact — it reports the acts the control requires and what the record does not hold. It has no permit template library, no scaffold/inspection certificates, no area-wide plant model, and no mobile offline capture.
- The whole suite is broadly red at base (stale SCHEMA_VERSION and spine pins across many inherited files); this session's focused suite plus the two neighbouring pin files it moved are green. Whole-suite status **[UNVERIFIED]**.

## Gaps named by this session

1. **Hardware isolation-verification** — a lock/device scan (Brady, ProLockout, VelocityEHS territory). The record carries the lock reference; it cannot prove the lock is on the hasp.
2. **Permit templates and scaffold/inspection certificates** — templated permit packs with per-kind mandatory sections.
3. **Shift handover of the plant state** — handovers are recorded per permit; an area-wide plant-state handover is not.
4. **Offline mobile capture at the point of work** — the proof is recorded by a person at a screen, not at the terminal.
5. **Area/plant model with interdependent isolations** — a group lockout whose removal requires every member's confirmation.

All five are logged in `ROADMAP.md` as unbuilt.
