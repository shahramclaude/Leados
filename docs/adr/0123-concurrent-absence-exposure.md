# ADR-0123 — Concurrent absence exposure: one window, several seats, one cover

- **Status:** accepted
- **Date:** 2026-11-07
- **Session:** 169 · Schema v115 (unchanged)
- **Deciders:** LeadOS maintainer; **director (rank 3) and VP (rank 4) workflow primary**, with mid-manager and frontline reading the same sweep inside their own span and the CEO/C-suite reading the enterprise sweep
- **Surface:** `#/duty-cover?panel=plan` (Absence plan panel → "Concurrent absence exposure" card), the register report, the upward brief and the duty-absence scan

## Context

ADR-0122 made an absence a declared ISO window on a seat and derived, on read, which duty lines lose their cover on those exact dates. It grades **one** absence against the declared covers. That is the frontline question ("I am out next week — who answers?"), and it is not the question the altitude above asks.

The real duty here is the director's: the last week of August, two of three branches have their shift lead out the same week, and the same manager is the only declared cover for both. Every per-absence reading is clean — each lead's window reads `covered` or `thin` against a named, eligible, exercised cover. The unit is not covered: one person is answerable for two absent seats on the same dates, and that person has no second hand. The register's own per-absence output cannot show this, because the collision exists only *between* two windows. A VP reading a function and a chief reading the enterprise hit the same blind spot one level up: simultaneous absence is the capacity question, and simultaneous absence plus a shared cover is the single point of failure.

The frameworks are named, not paraphrased: little's-law / queueing intuition in operations (a fixed cover pool facing concurrent demand loses capacity linearly per absence, not per person) — this module prints the counts, it does not forecast queue time; Jaques (1956) time-span of discretion and the ADR-0075 ladder — a cover answers only if their altitude carries the duty's horizon, which ADR-0121 already derives and this reading reuses; subsidiarity (Aghion & Tirole 1997) — the sweep is scoped to the seat's own span and rises to the lowest leadership ancestor whose span contains both windows, never sideways to a peer; Weick & Sutcliffe (2007) — a known collision that is written down before the week opens is a graceful degradation, while one discovered on the Tuesday is a surprise; Kahn (1990) — an availability nobody recorded is not an availability.

## Decision

**Concurrent absence exposure is a derived reading of the declared windows: the overlap is swept, the shared dates are printed, and the cover who would be the only answerable person for more than one absent seat is named — above rank 2 by rank and seat only.**

1. **Derived, never stored.** `absenceConcurrency(state, built, seat, {now, settings, items})` reads `absencePlans` and the declared `dutyCovers` only. Nothing about an overlap, a window band, an availability flag or a single-cover finding is written back to an absence row. The absence row keeps exactly what a human declared; the sweep re-derives on every read, so moving one window changes the reading on the next read and never leaves a stale verdict behind.
2. **Scoped to the seat's own span.** Participants are the active absence plans inside the acting seat's scope (full-access readers see the whole workspace); the acting seat's own window participates and is flagged `self`. A rank-1 lead therefore sees only their own window and gets a printed refusal — peer absence stays outside a peer's scope, unchanged from ADR-0121/ADR-0122. The overlap rolls up to the manager through the manager's own read of the same derived function, not through a copied record.
3. **Maximal shared ranges, inclusive, adjacency excluded.** Boundaries are each window's opening day and the day after it closes, so the sweep works on inclusive ISO day keys and never consults the clock. Adjacent ranges with an identical cast are merged into one window. `2027-07-05 → 07-06` and `2027-07-07 → 07-09` are **not** an overlap, and the reading prints that refusal rather than a zero.
4. **Graded on the shared dates, per duty line.** Each participant's duty lines are re-graded against the *shared* range through the existing `absenceWindowPlan` (not against the longer of the two absences), so the counts describe the days on which the seats are actually out together. The window band is `open-exposed` (some duty line has no available cover and carries open work), `exposed` (no available cover, nothing open today), `concentrated` (every line covered, but one person covers more than one absent seat) or `covered` (available, genuinely distinct covers). The premise that "exposed with nothing open" is still printed is deliberate: quiet is not safe.
5. **The single point of failure is computed, not guessed.** For each duty line the sweep takes the **available and eligible** declared covers; exactly one such cover makes that line a candidate. When the same person is the sole candidate for duty lines belonging to **two or more distinct absent seats in the same window**, they are printed as a single point of failure with their duty count, the number of absent seats and the seat refs. A cover who is themselves absent on the shared dates reads as a conflict (their line has no available cover), never as coverage.
6. **The identity wall is a single seam.** `namesVisible = fullAccess || rank ≤ 2`. Above rank 2 the cover's name and cover id are withheld while the seat label, rank, counts and the finding itself survive; absent-seat holders are rendered by seat ref. The sweep is redacted at construction, so the API, the report, the brief and the view cannot disagree about what a director may see.
7. **Printed refusals, capped honestly.** No absence declared, exactly one absence, and absences that never share a date each return a `refused` sentence explaining what could not be graded. The sweep grades at most `concurrentWindowCap` (24) overlapping windows and counts the remainder as `deferredWindowsN`; the brief and the card print the deferred count, so a large ledger cannot be summarised into one comfortable number.
8. **Wired into the same transaction and the same roll-up.** `dutyAbsenceDeclare` now computes the sweep against a hypothetical state (the new row not yet written) and puts the finding on the governed decision opened in the same `store.update` — "this window overlaps N other declared absence(s); on these dates N seats are absent at once, N duty lines have no available cover, and N declared cover(s) would be the only answerable person for two or more absent seats" — and on the audit event. The register report carries `absences.concurrency` at every altitude; the upward brief prints it; the absence scan records it. No new collection, no new rule id, no schema bump.

## Consequences

- The director, VP and chief can now see what the per-absence plan cannot: which weeks have less capacity than headcount suggests, and where one person is the whole plan for more than one absence.
- The reading is a register statement, not a score: it never rates a leader, never assigns a second cover, and never claims a collision will cause a failure. It prints the counts and the refusal.
- A second cover remains a human declaration (ADR-0121). The card says plainly that declaring a second cover removes the single point of failure and that the register will not assign one.
- Cost is bounded: the sweep is `windows × participants × duty lines` re-derivations over an already-harvested work index, capped at 24 windows.
- Limits, stated in the product and here: seats remain **workflow declarations, not authentication**; small counts can still be inferred; an "available" cover is available only in the sense that no absence is declared for them — a person pulled into another branch's incident is invisible to the register.
