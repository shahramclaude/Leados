# ADR-0242 — Lone-working welfare-check register: a declared interval, a clock that runs from the last contact, and an escalation addressed to the joint apex — never to the person it protects

- **Status:** accepted and built — Schema v196, route `#/lone-working`, `RULE-LWK-01` on the shared spine, CSV scope #183, demo seed, insights signals (LWK-01..04), focused suite 30/30, standalone rebuilt.
- **Date:** 2026-11-17
- **Deciders:** Somebody is working **alone**, at a declared site, inside a declared span, against a **written lone-working assessment**. The interval between contacts is a **decision somebody made about this task, this site and this person** — never a default. The welfare clock runs from **the last contact**, and time never closes anything. Every ask goes to the **joint apex** above every line that holds the person, and **never to the person themselves**, who is the one it protects. The strongest state this register will print is `welfare-unconfirmed`: a records system does not know where anybody is, and calling a colleague "missing" is a claim about the world it cannot make.
- **Schema:** **v196** (v195 → v196). Four additive collections, all **empty at upgrade**: `lwkDeployments`, `lwkCheckins`, `lwkEscalations`, `lwkMarkers`. No deployment, contact, escalation or marker is inferred from anything an upgraded workspace already holds — a shift on the roster is not a lone-working deployment, and a functional `lone-work` restriction (ADR-0241) is a limit somebody declared, not a person anybody is monitoring. Three bounded conventions: `lwkAutoPropagate` (pause switch), `lwkPrivacyFloor` 3 [2–20], `lwkHorizonDays` 30 [1–90].
- **Surface:** `#/lone-working` — Register (every deployment, its derived state at this minute, the exact late or overrun minutes, the declaring line beside the lines that hold the person), Checks (the contact record, per deployment, with the channel and the latency it implies), Lines (each active line's holding position and its open duties), Method (the instrument set, the vocabulary, and what the register refuses to do). Dialogs: declare deployment, record contact, raise escalation, close out. CSV #183 downloads from the register with its own descriptor.
- **Code:** `loneWorkingRegistry.js` (closed vocabularies, `LWK_STATES` ×10, `LWK_REFUSALS`, bounds, `LWK_CSV_COLUMNS` ×22, method copy), `loneWorkingSchema.js` (five fail-closed loaders + validators), `loneWorkingMath.js` (floor-only whole-minute kernels), `loneWorking.js` (pure reader, `deriveLwkState`, `loneWorkingAttentionCount`, LWK-01..04 signals), `loneWorkingActions.js` (declare, check-in, escalate, close out, set conventions, three reads, scan — all writes through `store.update`), `loneWorkingRules.js` (`RULE-LWK-01`), `loneWorkingReport.js` (identity-free CSV rows), `views/loneWorking.js` (five tabs + dialogs). ## Context

Lone working is the one safety control in the whole estate that is **administered by the person it protects**. The supervisor who sends somebody out alone owns a clock; when their shift ends, the clock does not end with them, and the person on the next shift inherits a duty nobody handed over. The benchmarked category — PeopleSafe, StaySafe, Blackline Safety, SoloProtect — and the UK HSE's lone-working guidance converge on the same three mechanics: a declared check-in interval, a timer running from the last contact, and an escalation when the timer expires **[vendor documentation; hands-on parity `[BENCHMARK-UNVERIFIED]`]**. The practitioner failure modes are equally consistent, and they are all *record* failures rather than app failures:

1. **The interval is a default.** A generic "check in every hour" applied to a two-hour substation task and to an eight-hour rural inspection is not a control; the interval is a decision about *this* task, and the record must show it was made and against what assessment.
2. **Contact is treated as an answer.** A person radios in to say they feel unwell and the box is ticked. The clock resets — correctly, because contact was made — but the concern nobody answered disappears, which is the exact sequence that precedes a fatality inquiry.
3. **The handover is where it dies.** The timer was set by the supervisor who went home. Nothing on the next shift's screen says the clock is theirs now, or that it expired forty minutes ago.
4. **"Missing" is asserted by a screen.** Products in the category lean on location and motion telemetry precisely because they cannot record a duty; the moment a dashboard prints "missing", it has made a claim about a human being's whereabouts that no records system is entitled to make.

Verified before building: word-boundary searches of `app/` returned **zero** files for `lone worker`, `lone working`, `welfare check`, `roll call`, `man-down`, `panic` and `buddy system`; the single `lone work` hit is ADR-0241's untestable functional domain. `#/lone-working` was unregistered, `RULE-LWK-01` did not exist, and CSV scope #183 was unused. The nearest neighbours each assume something this register does not: `stationCoverage` proves a *station* is manned, `dutyCoverage` proves a *duty* is covered while somebody is absent, `workRest` measures *rest between shifts*, and `workplaceAdjustment` records a limit but has no record of whether the person was ever alone. Wider semantic absence **[UNVERIFIED]**.

## Decision

**A deployment is declared against a written assessment with a decided interval and a declared span; the clock runs from the last contact in exact whole minutes; four different facts are printed separately and never folded into "checked in"; and every ask is addressed to the joint apex of the lines that hold the person.**

1. **Nothing is inferred, and a deployment is not a shift.** A deployment requires a person, a declared **site reference** (a place a supervisor wrote down — never a coordinate), a task description, a **written lone-working assessment** (≥12 characters and bounded), a whole-minute interval 5–1,440, a grace ≤120 minutes and a declared start and end. A deployment whose span is reversed, longer than 20,160 minutes, starts more than 365 days ahead or more than 365 days back, or overlaps another open deployment for the same person is **refused at the door with its reason**. The loader repairs nothing: an unreadable row is dropped with a counted warning, and an empty register prints as empty rather than as clear.

2. **The clock runs from the last contact, and time never closes anything.** `deadline = lastContactAt (or startAt) + interval`. Inside the grace the state is `not-yet-due`, then `check-due`; past the deadline plus grace with nothing on the record it is `welfare-unconfirmed`, carrying the **exact** minutes since the deadline. Past the declared end unclosed, it is `overran`, carrying the exact overrun minutes. A deployment whose declared end passed a year ago still reads `welfare-unconfirmed` at every later instant with nobody acting: there is no timer in this product and no state that a date change produces by itself.

3. **Four facts, never one word.** `welfare-unconfirmed` (nothing heard, nothing escalated), `escalation-open` (an escalation exists but welfare is still not confirmed), `welfare-confirmed-late` (welfare confirmed *after* the grace — the late minutes stay printed beside it, because the lateness happened), and `closed-without-contact` (closed out with no contact ever recorded, which the register cannot call a confirmed welfare) are **four separate states**. `checked-in` is printed only when contact landed inside the interval. Folding any of these into a green row would delete the only fact an inquiry asks for.

4. **Contact is not an answer.** A check-in carries one of two outcomes: `ok`, which resets the clock, and `concern`, which **also** resets the clock — a person who radios in to say they are unwell has made contact — but opens a duty (`concern-unanswered`) that only a recorded escalation closes. A concern cannot be closed by a later `ok`, by time, or by closing the deployment out; the state survives the close. `welfare-confirmed` is the only escalation outcome that clears `welfare-unconfirmed`, and it clears **nothing else**: the escalation record and its exact late minutes remain.

5. **The handover is on the record, per line.** A deployment belongs to a person and is declared by **one** line holder; the reader resolves **every** active line that holds the person today and prints the declaring line beside them, because "who sent them out" and "who answers for them now" are two different hands. A person is counted **once** in every people figure. The register is readable by any seat above the declaring line and by the person's own lines; a seat that holds neither sees counts only.

6. **The person is never asked, and nobody is scored.** A person may read their own deployment and may **not** declare one about themselves, nor escalate on their own behalf — both are refused with their own keys (`self-declaration`, `self-escalation`). The register counts deployments, contacts, late minutes and unconfirmed deployments; it never derives a per-person reliability figure, a "% checked in" ranking, or any named comparison. A safety control that produces a league table becomes a reason not to radio in.

7. **`RULE-LWK-01` asks, once, pausably, and only the hand that can act.** Three conditions: `welfare-unconfirmed`, `concern-unanswered`, and `overran`. One ask **per obligation** — per deployment and condition — with an opaque signature `lwk1:<kind>:<deploymentId>` that lives only in the `issues-obligation` link, addressed to `matrixJointApex` over every line holding the person. Idempotent per (signature, addressee), self-closing when the condition clears, capped at **4** new asks per commit with the remainder **counted as deferred**, governed by `lwkAutoPropagate` on every path including the manual scan, and never re-entered from an automation event. Where no legal addressee resolves — a person absent from the graph — the rule writes an **identity-free marker** and asks nobody: a guard against a malformed graph, asserted in the suite as unreachable from any legal roster.

8. **No location, no telemetry, no "missing".** The record holds a declared `siteRef` and a contact channel from a closed list of five (`radio`, `phone`, `app`, `in-person`, `system`), each with its own strength printed on screen — `system` is named the weakest, because a plant system reporting presence is not a human confirming welfare. No coordinate, no movement trail, no device identifier. The strongest adverse state is `welfare-unconfirmed`, and the Method tab says plainly that the register does not know where anybody is.

9. **The export is a count, never a person.** CSV #183 emits one summary row and, above the privacy floor, per-site and per-channel aggregate rows. No person id, no name, no manager id, no site reference, no task text and no timestamp of any individual contact crosses that boundary. A figure the reader cannot compute exports **blank with its reason**, never `0`. `balances` (states, contacts and escalations reconciling) is printed separately from `figuresComplete`/`incompleteReasons`, and the two are never collapsed into one claim.

## Consequences

- A workspace upgraded to v196 gains a register that is **honestly empty**, with its conventions declared and its first deployment refused until a supervisor writes the assessment the duty is declared against.
- The register's most valuable output is the **separation**: `concern-unanswered` and `welfare-confirmed-late` are facts no competing product prints, and both are exactly what a serious-injury inquiry asks first.
- Rank 1 may declare. The actor the register exists for is the first-line supervisor who can see the site; what protects the record is the written-assessment requirement, the `holdsPerson` reach, the refusal of self-declaration and the rule's joint-apex addressee — not a rank gate above the person who owns the clock.
- **Deliberately not built (scoped out this session, logged honestly):** no new `cases.js` link entity type, so a deployment is navigable from `#/lone-working` and from the apex's ask only — not from the person page or the case graph. No device integration, no paging gateway, no GPS, no map, and no automatic contact detection by design.
- **Asset blocker:** the Method tab's explanatory figure `app/assets/lone-working-welfare-method.jpg` was **not produced**. Higgsfield is not connected in this conversation, so the reference is reported as a blocker rather than filled with a placeholder or a fabricated diagram. The tab prints its method copy correctly without the figure; the image is the only missing piece and it is cosmetic, not evidentiary.
- **Benchmark `[BENCHMARK-UNVERIFIED]`:** the bar is taken from vendor documentation for PeopleSafe, StaySafe, Blackline Safety and SoloProtect, the HSE's lone-working guidance (INDG73) and BS 8484 for the device category. **Cleared natively:** declared interval, clock from last contact, escalation with a recorded response, shift-surviving record, identity-free export. **Exceeds:** the interval is declared against a written assessment per deployment; contact does not close a concern; late minutes survive confirmation; the ask goes to the joint apex across reporting lines and never to the person; no location claim and no "missing" state. No vendor environment was run and no hands-on parity is claimed.
