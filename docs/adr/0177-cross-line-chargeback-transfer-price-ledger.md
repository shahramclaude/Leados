# ADR-0177 — Cross-Line Labour Chargeback & Internal Transfer-Price Ledger (`RULE-TPL-01`)

- **Status:** Accepted and implemented (2026-11-11, Session 241)
- **Schema:** v142 (additive migration `141 → 142` adds two EMPTY collections, `transferPriceActs` and `transferPriceMarkers`, plus `settings.transferPriceAskDueDays` and `settings.transferPriceAutoPropagate`)
- **Altitude:** CEO / C-suite PRIMARY (the per-currency workspace statement and its zero-sum identity), with director / VP reading their span consolidated with eliminations and governing the scan and conventions, and frontline / mid-managers signing their own key and answering the asks.
- **Structure:** matrix PRIMARY. A single-chain workspace has no declared additional line, so the statement is `inert`: no row, no figure, no refusal and no ask.
- **Surface:** `#/matrix?panel=chargeback`; CSV scope `#132` (`line-chargeback`), aggregate and identity-free.

## Context

The matrix ledger knows what share of a person each line declared (ADR-0140) and the period-allocation ledger knows what share-days a period actually consumed (ADR-0158). The home-line history knows which manager was a person's home line on a given day (ADR-0149). Nothing priced any of it. A project line that drew 40% of a functional line's engineer for a quarter paid nothing: the lending line's budget carried the cost, the borrowing line's return was overstated by exactly that amount, and nobody at the top could say how much labour moved between lines, on whose terms, or whether the internal charges cancelled.

Frameworks applied:

- **Transfer pricing under decentralisation** (Hirshleifer, 1956): an internal price is what lets a division's own result steer it toward the enterprise optimum. A price of zero is still a price — the one that over-demands the shared resource.
- **The transfer-pricing problem as a management choice** (Eccles, 1985): cost, market and negotiated prices are different policies, so the basis is a declared label on every agreement and is never derived.
- **Responsibility accounting and consolidation:** a charge to one line is a credit to another, and charges between two lines inside one span are eliminated when that span is consolidated — which is why the workspace statement must net to exactly zero.
- **The tragedy of the commons** (Hardin, 1968) and **Coasean internal contracting** (Coase, 1937): a shared person with no agreed price is a commons; a two-key price is the smallest contract that ends it.

## Decision

1. **A price is a dated two-key agreement between two lines.** `transferPriceActs` holds append-only acts of three kinds: `propose` (first key), `confirm` (second key, stored with the exact terms it confirmed) and `retire` (with a reason). The **lender** is the home manager of the people being lent; the **borrower** is the manager who holds the declared additional line. Agreement states are derived on read and never stored: `proposed`, `agreed`, `declined` (retired by the counterpart), `withdrawn` (retired by the proposer), `voided` (an agreed price retired from a named day). Only an agreed price — or a voided one for the days before its `voidFrom` — prices anything. On upgrade the ledger is empty: no price is inferred.
2. **Exact integers, no rounding.** The rate is a whole number of currency units per full person-day (0 – 10,000,000), which makes one share-day (one hundredth of a person for one day) exactly that many cents. Every charge is `share-days × rate` in integer cents. A fractional rate (`600.50`) is refused, never rounded (`600.00` reads as the whole number 600); a blank is refused, never zero; an explicit `0` is a real two-key price and its share-days are counted apart. The currency is a three-letter code and is never defaulted. The basis is one of `standard-cost`, `market-rate` or `negotiated`. A price names both ends of its term (1–731 days); open-ended prices are refused.
3. **One price per directed pair per day.** A proposal whose term overlaps a live proposal or price for the same lender → borrower pair is refused at the door. If two agreed prices nonetheless cover one day (the door refuses it; an imported workspace can still carry it), the day is refused as `price-conflict`; neither is picked.
4. **Consumption comes from the period ledger, and the identity is checked.** Share-days are the consumed share-days of `buildLinePeriodAllocation` (ADR-0158) for the named period, walked day by day through the same share timeline (`shareTimelineFor` / `shareOnDay`), so a retune inside the period is priced piece by piece. `board.periodLedgerIdentity` asserts integer equality between the two ledgers. The period defaults to the last completed calendar quarter; an unreadable, reversed or out-of-bounds period is refused (`window-refused`), never clamped.
5. **The lender of each day is the recorded home line of that day.** From `buildStructureLedger` (ADR-0149): `observed` when a record covers the day; `presumed-current` when the person has no home-line history at all, read against today's manager and labelled on every figure; refused as `lender-unrecorded` when a history exists but does not cover the day (today's manager is not projected backwards) and as `lender-conflict` when two recorded periods cover it. Where the recorded home line is the borrower itself the share-days are `same-line`: not chargeable, counted, and not a refusal.
6. **Refusals are counted, never defaulted.** Each consumed share-day is in exactly one of `priced`, `no-agreed-price`, `awaiting-second-key`, `price-conflict`, `lender-unrecorded`, `lender-conflict` or `same-line`. No unpriced share-day is charged at zero or at an average of other pairs.
7. **Four claims, kept apart.** `shareDaysReconcile` (priced + refused + same-line = consumed), `periodLedgerIdentity` (these are the period ledger's figures), `zeroSum.holds` (total charged equals total credited and line nets sum to zero, per currency, as integer equality over the whole workspace) and `statementComplete` (every chargeable share-day in scope carries one two-key price). A statement that balances over the priced part is not reported as complete.
8. **Nets are withheld wherever a refusal touches them.** Each priced piece posts one charge to the borrower and the identical credit to the lender, in one currency; currencies are never pooled and no exchange rate is applied. A line's charged and credited amounts under the agreed prices are always printed. Its **net** is printed only when every share-day that names the line is priced and no share-day in the period has an unknown lender; otherwise it is `null`, rendered as the word "withheld" with its reason, and a blank CSV cell.
9. **Spans consolidate along the home-line tree with eliminations.** For every manager whose span holds more than its own account, charges between two lines inside the span are eliminated and only what crosses the boundary remains (`eliminated`, `inflow`, `outflow`, `net`). At the workspace root everything eliminates. A span's net is withheld under the same rule as a line's: it is printed only when every account in the span is priced in full and no share-day in the period has an unknown lender. An account whose manager also answers to a manager outside the span on a declared line is consolidated by the home-line convention and counted on the span row (`matrixSharedAccountsN`), so the convention is visible rather than silent.
10. **`RULE-TPL-01`** on the shared post-commit spine (110 rules / 110 contracts), after the co-hiring rule and before the seat roll-up rule. It fires on `transfer.price.proposed`, `transfer.price.confirmed`, `transfer.price.retired` and an explicit `transfer.price.scan`, and exits in O(1) while no price act exists — a workspace that never recorded a price is never nagged to start. Reading the whole-workspace statement for the last completed calendar quarter, it issues at most one dated delegation to the **named** manager who holds the missing key of each proposal, and at most one per (pair, quarter) to the **borrowing** manager of a pair that consumed share-days with no agreed price and no proposal covering them; a price between the pair that later dies (declined, withdrawn, voided) opens one more. Asks carry `issues-obligation` (the counterpart manager) and `rolls-up-to` (every roster ancestor of the asked manager in the matrix DAG) edges, are idempotent through `transferPriceMarkers`, are capped at eight per commit with the remainder counted and issued by a scan, and close themselves only when provably no longer owed, with the actual reason written on the task (the key was turned; the proposal was declined or withdrawn; every share-day of that pair's quarter is under a proposed or agreed price). An ask about a quarter that is no longer the last completed one is re-read against its own quarter and stays open while that quarter is unpriced — the calendar moving on is not an answer — and an ask still open keeps answering for its pair and quarter rather than being closed and reissued. A key held by the workspace leader or by a manager who has left the roster is recorded as an observation; nobody is substituted. The rule never signs a key, posts a charge, edits a budget or a line, or blocks a decision. Pausable with `transferPriceAutoPropagate`; an explicit scan always runs.
11. **Authority and scope.** `transferPricePropose`, `transferPriceConfirm` and `transferPriceRetire` require `agree-transfer-price` (rank 1–5). A declared seat turns only its own key — the lender key as the lending manager, the borrower key as the borrowing manager; there is no command that signs for a counterpart, and one manager signing twice is one key. The operator view may scribe either key but must name the side (`actingSide`); such acts are stored `via: 'operator'`, printed as scribed, and an agreement whose two keys were both scribed is counted apart. The normalizer re-checks on load that the second key is on the other side, carries the proposal's exact terms and, when signed from a seat, was signed from the seat that holds it; anything else is dropped and counted. `transferPriceScan` and `transferPriceSetConventions` require `govern-transfer-prices` (rank 3–5) or the operator view. `transferPriceReport` is a safe read scoped inside the read model: a seat reads the lines it holds or spans and the counterparty of each of its own pairs; names outside that are withheld; rows, accounts and agreements outside the seat are counted, not shown; and a seat's own per-currency position is not presented as a zero-sum claim.

## Not in this slice

- **Posting.** This is a statement, not a posting: no budget envelope, actual or forecast (ADR-0142) is written. Charging a budget line with these figures is a later, separate human act.
- **Rate cards.** One price per directed pair per day; rates by grade, role, line kind or individual declaration are not modelled, so two people lent between the same two lines share one rate.
- **Hours.** Share-days are declared allocation over calendar days, not hours worked.
- **People who have left the roster.** They are outside the period ledger's population, so their past share-days are counted as engine refusals and not priced.
- **Foreign exchange.** Currencies are reported side by side; no conversion or consolidated total across currencies exists.
- **A settings form for `transferPriceAskDueDays`.** The value is stored, bounded 1–30 (out-of-bounds input is refused, not clamped) and honoured; it is set through `transferPriceSetConventions` and has no dedicated control yet.
- **Authentication.** Seats are workflow declarations. A key turned from a seat records which seat was declared, not who was at the keyboard.

## Consequences

- A line that borrows people can no longer consume them for a closed quarter without that fact being printed as refused share-days and put in front of the borrowing manager, once, with a due date.
- The top of the house can read, per currency, what was charged and credited between lines and whether it nets to zero — and can see separately whether that statement covers the whole period or only the part that happens to be priced.
- A workspace that never records a price, and every single-chain workspace, behaves exactly as before.
- Voiding an agreed price from a past day restates every period it covered. The void is a dated act with a reason, is refused by default for a term that has already ended unless the day is named, and the share-days it uncovers are refused until a new price is agreed.
