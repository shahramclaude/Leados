# ADR-0262 — Secondment / Assignment Return & Handover Register

**Date:** 2026-11-18 · **Status:** Accepted for canonical source · **Surface:** `#/secondment` · **Storage:** v213 → v214 · **Spine:** RULE-ASG-01 (#170) · **CSV:** #198.

## Decision

A mid-manager whose person moves across lines for a period should not have to trust a calendar reminder. The workspace moves duties well — delegations, portfolio handovers, shift handovers — and records people starts and exits, but it never recorded the ACT itself: a person taken from one line to another for a period, the duties they carried and who covered each one, the decision that had to be made BEFORE the declared return day, and the handback evidence from both lines. `goalCommitmentRegistry.js` prints the absence in its own prose: a secondment "does not exist in this workspace yet". The roadmap logged this gap at Session 312 and left it Planned. This register closes it: **the return decision is a dated act with a clock — extend, return or convert — and nothing is ever ended, extended, converted or transferred by silence.** Primary altitude **mid-manager / director with VP oversight** × **single-chain and matrix** (a secondment names two sides, so every question routes to the joint apex above both — never one manager on another's behalf). The real duty: a hand declares the assignment and the window, the home line names coverage per duty, a hand records the return decision with written rationale before the declared day, the home line consents by its own dated act before a conversion completes, and BOTH lines evidence the handback.

## Evidence and calculation

- **The return decision is an act, not a column.** An assignment binds a person, two named sides (home manager, host manager), a start day, a declared return day, a terms instrument and the recording hand. The return-decision chain is a ledger: `extend` answers the question in force and opens a new one at a later declared day (only ever forward, past both the day in force and the decided day); `return` and `convert` answer it finally and carry no new day. Each decision's timeliness is judged against the day in force AT its own decided day; a decision recorded after that day keeps its own day and prints `decided-late` with the exact whole days. The ORIGINAL day is kept and printed beside the day in force, so drift is visible rather than absorbed. The declared day is never moved to make a late act timely.
- **Nine derived states, nothing stored.** `planned`, `decision-due` (day in force inside the declared warn horizon), `return-decision-overdue` (day passed unanswered — the exact days print, and the assignment is neither ended nor extended nor converted by the passage of time), `active`, `handback-open` (decided `return`, a handback missing), `returned` (decided `return`, BOTH lines' evidence), `transfer-consent-owed` (decided `convert`, home consent missing), `transfer-handback-open` (consent on file, a handback missing), `transferred` (consent AND both handbacks). Every state is recomputed on read from the declared window, the act ledger and the injected clock; a stored record carries no state, no verdict and no count, so a hand-edited file cannot carry an assignment marked "returned" whose ledger holds no handback.
- **Coverage is named per duty, never inferred.** Each declared duty carries one of three kinds: `named-cover` (a named hand), `retained-by-home` (the home line keeps it), or `uncovered` — printed as a hole and asked about, never smoothed over. Coverage is never read off goals, delegations or org position; the latest declaration per duty governs and earlier rows stay as history.
- **A conversion never completes silently.** `convert` needs the home line's own dated consent — the consenting hand must BE the home manager or a hand proved strictly above them on the live graph — and both handbacks before the state moves. There is no command that sets a state, ends an assignment, moves `person.managerId`, re-dates a decision or scores a person. The roster change a completed transfer implies is a separate human act in the matrix module.
- **Matrix correctness (ADR-0140/0141).** The reader resolves every ACTIVE line holding the person through the ONE matrix engine, counts the person ONCE, and derives the joint apex over every hand that answers for the move: the two named sides and every active line's manager. An off-graph named hand is printed as an orphan rather than dropped; a named side that no longer holds an active line prints `side-line-mismatch` — the declaration and the live graph disagree, and the register never silently repoints a declaration at a manager nobody named. Every write is narrowed to a seat that HOLDS a named side (`side-not-held`), and each act on a side is narrowed to a seat holding THAT side.
- **RULE-ASG-01** (spine #170, `asgAutoPropagate`) files at most one dated ask per condition per joint seat, capped at 4 per commit with the remainder counted: `return-decision-due`, `return-decision-overdue`, `duty-cover-uncovered`, `handback-incomplete` (past the declared handback horizon), `transfer-consent-owed` (move due or past due). Every ask goes to the **lowest joint apex above every hand that answers for the move**; where nobody can be asked the rule writes ONE identity-free `asgMarkers` observation per condition and substitutes nobody. Ask text is deliberately opaque (a secondment schedule names people and duties, and a delegation is readable above the addressee); the `asg1:<kind>:<assignmentId>[:dutyLabel]` signature lives only in the typed `issues-obligation` link. Asks self-close when the condition clears and answered/closed asks are never re-filed; pause-governed on every path, including the explicit scan.

## Boundaries and costs

- Four collections, all empty at upgrade (`asgAssignments`, `asgCovers`, `asgActs`, `asgMarkers`) + four bounded conventions (`asgAutoPropagate` defaulted ON, `asgPrivacyFloor` 3, `asgWarnHorizonDays` 14, `asgHandbackHorizonDays` 14). **Nothing is inferred** at upgrade: manufacturing a secondment from an org chart would put a move on the record no hand declared. Loaders are fail-closed with printed drop reasons (a bad window, a person as their own side, one hand as both sides, a second answer to an answered question, an extend that does not move the day forward, a consent with no convert on the chain, a handback with no decision); every write runs the loader and stores the row the loader returns. An off-roster hand is KEPT and printed as an orphan — deleting it would erase a live secondment duty.
- Read/write reach is recomputed on the live reporting graph (`read-secondment` / `manage-secondment`, rank 2–5). Above rank 4 the reader prints counts and withholds hands and duty detail (manifest class `portfolio`, detailCeiling 4); the register is deliberately NOT an individual route. The CSV (#198 `secondment`) is aggregate, identity-free and floored — counts and published-vocabulary keys only; a per-state count below the declared privacy floor exports BLANK with its reason, never as a small number, while a zero prints as zero (absence is a fact). The register scores and ranks **nothing and nobody**.
- **Asset provenance (mandate):** Higgsfield is not connected in this environment (`list_connector_tools('higgsfield')` returns `unsupported`, platform-wide — the standing S323–S331 blocker, reported not papered over). The method illustration `app/assets/secondment-method.jpg` was generated with Arena `generate_image` and is labelled on screen as illustrative, not operational evidence. ## Benchmark and remainder

The specialist bar is SAP SuccessFactors Global Assignments (home + host records, dual managers) and the assignment-management systems of record Topia, Equus, Workia and Benivo (letters, tax/immigration workflows, audit trails) [1](https://www.sap.com/products/hcm/global-assignments.html) [2](https://www.topia.com) — same-input trials **[BENCHMARK-UNVERIFIED]** (this sandbox reached public comparison pages only; no live vendor workspace was reachable). What none of them does is make the **return decision a dated, timeliness-judged act that nothing can auto-answer**, enforce **home consent before a conversion completes**, require **both lines' handback evidence**, route **joint-apex asks with person-once and orphan handling**, print **uncovered duties as holes**, and floor its **aggregate export**. Weaker than those tools on the tax/immigration corpus, payroll/cost estimates, day-count tracking, assignment letters/e-signature and HRIS integration — a specialist import is the honest answer there, and the register refuses to fake a tax question it cannot evidence.

**In Progress / unbuilt (logged in ROADMAP.md):** a tax/immigration corpus for assignments (refused as fake precision without a real corpus); assignment letters/e-signature; the related-party transaction & disclosure register is the new gap. Nothing here is HR advice or a legal finding; it is the record that lets a mid-manager see who moved, what is owed before the declared day, and what both lines still have to hand back.

## Verification notes (defects caught in this build's own code)

Three real defects were found by the focused test pass and fixed before ship: `asgMath.js` used `extends` as an identifier (a strict-mode reserved word — the module would not parse at all); `normalizeAsgSettings` read defaults through the setting key instead of the bound short key, so every numeric convention defaulted to `undefined` and no assignment could ever print `decision-due`; and both this register's view and ADR-0261's view called `table()` with string columns, where the component contract is object columns with `render`/`key` — every cell rendered `—`. The last defect was an ADR-0261 leftover too and is repaired there. Standing ADR-0261 repairs delivered in the same pass: the cal seat gate now fails closed on a truthy `{allow:false}` verdict with no first-seat fallback; `calAutoPropagate` and the cal rights are registered in the pause census and API maps; `performance-calibration` joins the disclosure-compiler renderer census. Details in ADR-0261's repair note.
