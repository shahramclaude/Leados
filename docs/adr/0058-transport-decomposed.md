# ADR-0058 — Transport, decomposed: two axes, three landmarks, challenged freezes

**Status:** Accepted · 2026-10-31 · Session 95 · No schema change · SURV-11 · RULE-SURV-08

## Decision

Session 94 answered "which exit moved?" at one landmark. Two questions remained open and both were already leaking into other modules. First: a day-30 verdict is one point on a curve — a shift visible only by day 14, or only by day 60, is a different operational fact. Second: `waitCost.rowConfidence` downgraded *any* class with `drift.changed` to low confidence, which is the pooled any-exit answer applied to a question with two axes. The Cost of waiting's exposure arithmetic is timing arithmetic (any exit ends the wait); its overdue framing rides on the completion median. A substitution undermines the second and leaves the first standing, yet the old code punished both — and, symmetrically, an exit-mix shift with unchanged timing could never downgrade anything through that path at all, because `drift.changed` was false.

LeadOS now runs the decomposition at three landmarks and reads stability as **two axes**:

- **Landmarks 14 / 30 / 60.** `driftCohorts` is generalized to `driftCohortsAt(items, key, today, τ)`: recent cohort started [τ, τ+60) days ago, prior [τ+60, τ+120), follow-up capped at τ. Day 30 is byte-identical to the Session-94 definition (pinned by a deep-equality test and by Session 94's unchanged suite). The 14/60 flanks form the **landmark profile** (`driftLandmarkProfile`) with their own fixed 24-test Holm family (6 classes × 2 causes × 2 landmarks). Three families now run per scan — drift (6), day-30 decomposition (12), profile (24) — each controlled within itself. They share cohorts and are positively dependent; joint control across families and across repeated scans is **not** claimed, and the UI says so. The pattern across landmarks is named as a shape (`flat`, `early-only`, `mid-only`, `late-only`, `multi-landmark`, `sustained`, `crossing`, `not-assessed`): a change significant only at day 14 reads as intake volatility; one that survives to day 60 is structural; a cause significant in opposite directions at different landmarks is `crossing` and is named rather than averaged. The profile never opens work — `RULE-SURV-07` remains driven by day 30 alone.
- **Transport axes.** `classTransport(klass)` is a total function over a class row returning timing (the calendar-cohort any-exit log-rank), completion (the day-30 verdict), the landmark shape, and a headline (`both-undermined`, `completion-only`, `timing-only`, `transport-supported`, `not-assessed`). Favorable verdicts are `favorable-shift`, never a failure: the pooled curve reads conservatively, and that is stated. A class is `not-assessed` when neither axis could speak — the word "stable" is never printed on an untested class. This is named in the code and the UI as what it is: a **descriptive stability audit of a reference class**. It is not transportability in the Bareinboim–Pearl sense — no selection diagram, no causal estimand is transported anywhere — and no number is ever recalibrated. An axis failing means "stop quoting these outputs", not "the curve was adjusted".

Downstream, the axes are consumed where numbers are actually read:

- **`waitCost.rowConfidence`** caps a row at low only when the *timing* axis is undermined. Under a completion-only failure the level stays evidence-based and the basis names exactly what not to quote (completion median, overdue framing) and what stands (weekly exposure, still-open timing). Under a favorable shift the basis says the pooled baseline is conservative.
- **The Consequence Lab** shows a caution banner on the delay futures when the decision class's timing axis is undermined (integrated costs are descriptive until reviewed) and an informational one when only completion moved (these any-exit futures stand). Frozen consequence readings stamp both axes into `graphSnapshot` at freeze time, so a later Brier grade records whether the curve was already in question. The stamps are whitelist-normalized additively — no schema-version bump.
- **SURV-11** fires only in the completion-only case: it is the sentence "completion forecasts no longer transport — timing still does".
- **CSV scope #53** gains eleven columns (transport axis codes, shape, day-14/60 deltas and Holm p) under the same opacity rules: blank where refused, never zero, no titles.

**`RULE-SURV-08`** makes frozen evidence find out. On `survival.scan`, every frozen survival reading with predictions in a class whose completion or timing axis is undermined receives an appended **transport-challenge note** (which axis, why, how many predictions ride on it) — the frozen probabilities are never rewritten. Every frozen wait-exposure certificate is challenged *only* when the timing axis fails, because a certificate prices any-exit waiting; the newest affected certificate per class gets one owned re-freeze task, typed-linked `tracks-followup`. A completion-only failure deliberately leaves certificates standing — that discrimination is the test `completion-only failure: the reading is challenged, the certificate is left alone`. Caps (5 notes, 3 tasks per scan), markers per rule × artefact × class × axis × ISO week, marker history sliced to 400.

## Transactional consequences

None beyond the existing spine. Profile and transport are pure read models attached as `klass.profile` / `klass.transport`; RULE-SURV-08 reuses `survivalMarkers` and existing record shapes (reading notes, delegations, typed links), so there is **no schema change**. The consequence snapshot stamps are additive whitelist fields in an existing normalizer. Floors and family sizes are named constants (`DRIFT_LANDMARKS`, `DRIFT_PROFILE_FAMILY_SIZE`). The day-30 decomposition was refactored through a shared `decomposeLandmark` used by both the verdict path and the profile, so the three landmarks cannot drift apart in implementation.

## Alternatives rejected

- **One unified 36-test Holm family across 14/30/60.** It would either change Session-94 day-30 verdict p-values retroactively or force the same day-30 cell to display two different adjusted p-values in two places. Keeping day 30 as its own family preserves the verdict path exactly; the flanks are diagnostic.
- **Gray's (1988) k-sample test now.** Still deferred for the Session-94 reason: its influence-function variance cannot yet be verified against a published example inside this codebase, and a p-value LeadOS cannot defend is worse than a named gap. The multi-landmark profile covers the shape question descriptively in the meantime.
- **Recalibrating the pooled curve on the recent cohort.** Sixty days of one class is not enough data to overwrite a reference class, and silent adjustment would destroy the audit trail the frozen readings exist to create. Labelling beats adjusting.
- **Challenging exposure certificates on completion failure too.** That is precisely the pooled thinking this session removes: the certificate's arithmetic is any-exit and remains valid. Opening re-freeze work there would be noise.
- **A transport "score".** A single 0–100 stability number would pool the two axes back into one verdict and hide exactly the discrimination the feature exists to make.

## Consequences and limits

Association only, classes not people, as with every survival feature. The profile reuses start cohorts whose windows overlap across landmarks, so the three landmarks are dependent views of partly the same items — the shape is a description, not three independent tests. Case-mix overlap (positivity) remains unadjusted and is now the sharpest open item on the roadmap, together with a repeated-look error budget and durable statistical review snapshots. "Supported" means "neither audit found a shift above its floors", not equivalence. Marker history is capped at 400 tags, so a very old challenge can repeat after ~400 newer markers; it would re-append a note to a still-frozen reading, an accepted eventual behavior shared with the other survival rules. Real-browser rendering beyond JSDOM + axe is [UNVERIFIED].

## Validation

`tests/app/drift-transport.test.mjs` (19 tests): τ=30 cohort deep-equality with the Session-94 definition; follow-up capping at the flanks (post-τ exit becomes censoring at τ; too-young items excluded, never extrapolated); substitution significant at day 14 under the 24-family with the identity exact; day-60 refusal with counts; a late-only fixture silent at 14/30 and named at 60; Holm monotonicity over the profile family; `profileShape` over fabricated blocks (flat / early-only / sustained / crossing / not-assessed); `classTransport` for substitution (completion-only), timing-only drift, improvement (favorable-shift), and totality on fabricated input; SURV-11 firing only completion-only; wait-cost confidence keeping its level under completion-only failure while naming the framing, and capping at low under timing failure; RULE-SURV-08 challenging a reading without touching its frozen probabilities, leaving the certificate alone under completion-only failure, opening exactly one linked re-freeze task under timing failure, refusing graded readings and untouched classes, replay empty; consequence stamps built, normalized, and cleared when invalid; CSV transport/profile columns measured-or-blank with no titles; the transport card rendered with a labelled, well-formed three-landmark table and the method panel's named non-claims. Session 94's decomposition suite and all pre-existing suites are unchanged and green.
