# ADR-0057 — Decompose delivery drift by cause instead of pooling exits

**Status:** Accepted · 2026-10-31 · Session 94 · No schema change · SURV-09/10 · RULE-SURV-07

## Decision

The calendar-cohort drift guardrail tests time to *any* exit. That is the right transport question and the wrong management question: a class can exit at unchanged speed while completions are one-for-one replaced by abandonments, and every any-exit view (cycle time included) stays green. LeadOS now decomposes the drift at the same fixed cohorts and the same equal 30-day follow-up into its two competing causes, and lets the component that moved choose the intervention.

The decomposition is arithmetic on an identity, not a model. `kaplanMeier` now materialises **both** Aalen–Johansen incidences, so at every step `S + F_complete + F_abandon = 1` exactly; at the day-30 landmark the change in "still open" is therefore the completion change plus the abandonment change with no remainder (`identityResidual` is computed and tested, not asserted). Each cause is compared across cohorts with a two-sample **Wald landmark test** on the incidence difference using the estimator's delta-method variances, Holm-controlled across the fixed a-priori family of 6 classes × 2 causes — a second family beside the drift family's six, labelled as such; the scan-wise error across both families is not controlled and the page does not claim otherwise. The Pepe & Mori (1993) integrated curve difference is printed as a descriptive effect size in incidence-days, never with a p-value.

Three honesty mechanisms are load-bearing:

1. **Sparse-cause refusal.** A cause with fewer than five pooled events inside 30 days is reported as counts with a reason. No share, no p-value. A cohort below eight items refuses the whole decomposition, mirroring the drift floor.
2. **Boundary discipline.** At an incidence of exactly 0 or 1 in both cohorts the Wald variance is truly zero. Identical shares are p = 1 (not a refusal); differing shares fall back to **Fisher's exact test** on the day-30 counts *only when both cohorts are verified fully observed to day 30* (censoring only at the landmark — checked from the rows, never assumed, because a record parked mid-window is censored early and Fisher would then be biased); otherwise the cause is refused.
3. **A corrected variance.** Implementing the decomposition exposed that the cumulative-incidence variance shipped in Session 89 was a mis-transcription: a Y² denominator where the delta method requires Y(Y−d), the total exit count where the cause count belongs, the −2 cross term missing, and the final F referenced at every step instead of F(t). It understated the variance when events were lumped (0.0048 where the truth is 0.012) and overstated it when spread (0.0344). It is replaced by the three-term delta-method form, chosen over Aalen's counting-process form because the latter overestimates at workspace sample sizes (Braun & Yuan 2007). The anchor is exact and pinned: with no censoring before t the estimator is a multinomial proportion and the variance collapses to F(1−F)/n, lumped or spread. Session 89's incidence bands and quantile brackets now use the corrected standard errors; no frozen reading is rewritten, because readings store probabilities, not bands.

`RULE-SURV-07` turns an adverse attribution into the week's single drift decision, framed by component: abandonment-led verdicts (substitution, abandonment-rise, churn) open an **intake review** with the ten most recent abandonments linked as exhibits; completion-led verdicts (completion-decline, stall) open a **completion review** about blockers and capacity. Exactly one drift decision exists per class per week: if `RULE-SURV-06`'s generic transport review already exists, the decomposition attaches its owned investigation and links to that decision (found by the frozen tag in its rationale); if the decomposition fires first, `RULE-SURV-06` stands down. A significant improvement opens one owned task to record what changed — positive deviance is evidence too. Signals `SURV-09` (adverse attribution) and `SURV-10` (substitution *without* any-exit drift — the case no other signal can see) join the existing Insights bucket. CSV scope #53 gains the decomposition columns under the same opacity rules: class code, cohort counts, incidences, deltas, Holm p, verdict code; blank wherever refused.

## Transactional consequences

None beyond the existing spine. The decomposition is a pure read model recomputed on read (`driftDecomposition`, attached as `klass.decomp` beside `klass.drift`); the rule reuses `survivalMarkers`, so there is **no schema change** — the same precedent the drift guardrail itself set. Floors are named exported constants (`DRIFT_MIN_COHORT`, `DRIFT_MIN_CAUSE_EVENTS`, `DRIFT_FAMILY_SIZE`), mirroring the drift test's literals. All writes remain marker-idempotent per class × ISO week; an explicit scan is never suppressed by the pause setting, as with every survival rule.

## Alternatives rejected

- **Gray's (1988) k-sample test now.** Its modified risk sets retain items after a competing exit, and its variance is not the log-rank variance on those sets. Approximating it would print a p-value the codebase cannot defend. The landmark test's variance is exact from the estimator already on the page; curve-wide shape differences that cancel by day 30 are described by the integrated difference instead. Gray with a verified variance remains planned.
- **Fine–Gray subdistribution regression.** Already rejected in Session 89; nothing about two fixed cohorts needs a proportional-hazards assumption on top.
- **Gating the decomposition on the any-exit drift firing.** That hierarchy would miss the substitution case, which is the point of the feature.
- **A schema bump with stored decomposition readings.** The drift guardrail set the precedent: recompute on read, freeze only into decision text. Stored readings would invite back-filled "predictions" of a diagnostic.
- **Seeding backdated history into the demo so the card lights up.** The demo is a current workspace; mature cohorts refuse with the counts printed ("recent 4, prior 3"), exactly as the drift check does. Forty fabricated historical records shared by ~96 suites would be churn in service of a screenshot.

## Consequences and limits

Association only: a verdict attributes a recorded shift to finishing versus giving up; it never says why, and no person is scored — cohorts are classes of work. The landmark is day 30 by construction; a shift that lives entirely after day 30 is invisible here and visible in the class curve. Case mix is unadjusted (the positivity/overlap check remains planned), repeated scans are not sequentially error-budgeted (also planned), and "no attributable shift" is not equivalence. The two Holm families are controlled separately at 0.05 per scan. Real-browser rendering of the new card beyond JSDOM + axe is [UNVERIFIED].

## Validation

`tests/app/drift-decomposition.test.mjs` (19 tests): the partition identity at every step; the multinomial variance anchor lumped *and* spread; Fisher pinned to three published values (tea-tasting 0.0285714, 0.485714, the R textbook 0.002759); the silent-substitution fixture (drift `changed: false`, verdict `substitution`, residual 0, integrated ±18.5 incidence-days by hand); completion-decline, abandonment-rise, broad-improvement, sparse and thin refusals; Holm monotonicity; report/signal/brief wiring with a title-leak negative; both SURV-06/SURV-07 precedence directions; timing-only drift still receiving the generic review (no regression); the learning task; a real `createApi.scanSurvival()` commit persisted across reload; CSV opacity and blanks; the classes-panel card with one cell per header and the method panel's named non-claims.
