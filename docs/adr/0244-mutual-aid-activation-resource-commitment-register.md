# ADR-0244 — Mutual-Aid Activation & Resource-Commitment Register: the bilateral agreement, the explicit acceptance with its written confirmation and promise, the arrival compared line by line, and the withdrawal-then-release ordering — printed, never repaired

- **Status:** accepted and built — Schema v198, route `#/mutual-aid`, `RULE-MAA-01` on the shared spine, CSV scope #185, demo seed, insights signals (MAA-01..04), focused suite 20/20.
- **Date:** 2026-11-17
- **Deciders:** A plant is flooded; the workspace has a mutual-aid agreement with a neighbouring plant and asked for help two weeks ago. Nobody can reconstruct **what the partner promised, whether it arrived, and in what order the help left and the commitment was released** — because the workspace can store an incident and an action, but cannot store the outward mutual-aid transaction itself. This register exists to answer one question: **what was promised in writing, what actually arrived against that promise, and has the commitment been closed in the order the register requires?**

## Context

Mutual aid is the outward transaction every incident plan assumes and almost no workspace can prove. The FEMA mutual-aid system and the EMAC interstate compact rest on the same four objects — a bilateral agreement, a request, a written acceptance with the resources promised and the day they arrive, and the release that closes the commitment **[FEMA/EMAC published practice; hands-on parity `[BENCHMARK-UNVERIFIED]`]**. The UK LRF/RELIEF mutual-aid framework adds the same discipline to the local-resilience side. The practitioner failure modes are record failures, not app failures:

1. **A request stands in for a promise.** The workspace asked for help and the help arrived; nobody can show what the partner actually committed to. A request is an ask; an acceptance is a promise; the register refuses to let the first stand in for the second.
2. **The promise is a name and a number.** "Two pumps and a duty officer" recorded in a note cannot be compared against what actually arrived, and a shortfall is discovered as a surprise, not as a fact with days attached.
3. **Arrival is recorded as a feeling.** A "done" tick cannot say whether the arrival matched the promise line by line, or how many days late it was.
4. **The commitment is closed in the wrong order.** The help leaves and the release is recorded later — or the release is recorded first. The order is the commitment's integrity, and nothing derived it.
5. **The breach is repaired away.** A release dated before a withdrawal, a cancellation dated after an acceptance, a request made under an agreement that had expired: the loader's job is to carry what the record says, not to clean it up.

## Decision

**The register stores the bilateral agreement, the request with its declared validity, the partner's EXPLICIT acceptance with its written confirmation and promised resources and arrival day, the arrival compared line by line against the promise, and the withdrawal-then-release ordering — derived on every read, printed with exact days, never repaired, never inferred.**

1. **A request is never a promise.** Three distinct states separate the ask from the commitment: `requested`, `awaiting-acceptance`, and `request-lapsed` (the declared validity closed with no acceptance — the ask died, and nothing was promised). The partner's acceptance is a separate dated act requiring a **written confirmation reference**, the **hand that accepted**, the **promised arrival day** and the **promised resource lines**. Nobody may record an acceptance, an arrival, a withdrawal or a release against a request that has none.
2. **Four refusals the door enforces.** A request with no declared validity is refused; an acceptance with no written confirmation is dropped by the loader; an arrival without a promise is refused (`acceptance-not-found`); a release without a withdrawal is refused (`no-withdrawal`); a withdrawal without a release is not refused — it is a printed state with a due day (`withdrawn`). A request made under an agreement whose term has expired is refused at the door (`agreement-lapsed`) and, if imported, printed as a breach.
3. **The arithmetic is exact and whole-day.** Validity closes on `openedOn + validityDays`; overdue days, late days and release grace are whole days; the coverage comparison is an exact integer per resource kind (asked vs promised vs arrived), never a percentage and never a rounded figure. A span is floored, so the register never claims a day that has not finished.
4. **Fourteen request states, and the breaches are kept.** `requested`, `awaiting-acceptance`, `request-lapsed`, `cancelled`, `accepted-differently` (the promise does not match the ask — the exact shortfall is printed), `awaiting-arrival`, `not-arrived-overdue` (the promised day passed), `arrived-complete`, `arrived-partial`, `arrived-late`, `withdrawn` — plus three breach states reachable **only** via imported or hand-edited data: `breach-agreement-lapsed`, `breach-release-before-withdrawal`, `breach-cancelled-after-acceptance`. The loader keeps them, the reader prints them with their exact days, and nothing repairs them.
5. **Withdrawal then release, in that order.** The help leaves (a withdrawal with its day) and the commitment is released after it. A release dated before the withdrawal is a breach the door cannot create and the loader will not erase. The release ask fires from the withdrawal's fields: a withdrawal with no release past the declared grace is a live obligation even when the arrival state has already printed, because the commitment has not been closed.
6. **The joint seat holds the act, and one line cannot act for two.** A request names the requesting lines whose work it commits. Every act on it — acceptance, arrival, withdrawal, release, cancellation — is narrowed inside the action to a seat that HOLDS one of the requesting lines through the span or a declared line; where two or more lines requested, the act belongs to the **joint apex** above them, computed through `matrixJointApex`, never to one line acting for another.
7. **`RULE-MAA-01` asks, once, pausably, and only the hand that can act.** Four conditions: `request-lapsed` (validity closed, no acceptance — the ask goes to the joint apex above the requesting lines, **never** to the partner), `not-arrived-overdue` (accepted, promised day passed, no arrival), `withdrawn-not-released` (the release is owed), and `breach` (any of the three breach states). One ask per obligation with an opaque signature `maa1:<condition>:<id>` living only in the `issues-obligation` link. Idempotent per (signature, addressee), self-closing when the condition clears, capped at **4** new asks per commit with the remainder counted as deferred, governed by `maaAutoPropagate` on every path including the manual scan. Where the requesting lines share no manager, the rule writes an **identity-free marker** and asks nobody: an obligation to a node that holds no line is an obligation to nobody.
8. **Reading is open; the reader withholds identity above rank 3.** Every seat may see whether the workspace owes or is owed mutual aid and where a commitment stands, so the read right is rank 1. Above rank 3 the reader withholds partner identity, agreement references, subjects, confirmations, external hands and resource lines — what a partner promised, in its own words, is partner material. Writing is a rank 2–5 leader act narrowed inside the action.
9. **The export is a count, never a partner or a person.** CSV #185 emits one summary row plus one row per printed state. No partner name, no agreement reference, no subject, no confirmation, no external hand, no resource line and no day crosses that boundary. A per-state count below the privacy floor exports **blank with its reason**, never `0`; `complete` is separate from the counts and from `balances`, and the claims are never collapsed into one.

## Consequences

- A workspace upgraded to v198 gains a register that is **honestly empty**, with its three conventions declared and its first agreement refused until somebody writes the bilateral instrument the duty rests on.
- The register's most valuable output is the **exact comparison**: `accepted-differently` prints the shortfall per resource kind; `arrived-late` prints the late days; `breach-release-before-withdrawal` prints the days the order was inverted — the three facts a partner dispute, an insurance file or a debrief asks for first, and that almost nothing in the category records.
- The demo seed exercises the whole vocabulary on first load: two agreements (one expired), six requests, four acceptances, one arrival, two withdrawals and no releases — deriving `request-lapsed`, `accepted-differently` (a two-pump ask promised one), `not-arrived-overdue`, `arrived-complete`, `withdrawn`, `cancelled`, with `statesReconcile: true`.
- **Deliberately not built (scoped out this session, logged honestly):** no automatic partner discovery; no calendar-based resource matching; no per-person or per-partner scoring; no standing reciprocity ledger — the register is transaction-scoped, and a term-level net position across partners is a separate capability, logged in `ROADMAP.md` as planned.
- **Method figure:** the Method tab prints its framework basis and engine version as text. No explanatory image was produced this session; the figure is cosmetic, not evidentiary, and is reported as missing rather than faked.
- **Benchmark `[BENCHMARK-UNVERIFIED]`:** the bar is taken from FEMA mutual-aid agreement practice, the EMAC offer-and-acceptance contract principles, and the UK LRF/RELIEF mutual-aid framework as published. **Cleared natively:** a bilateral agreement, a request with a declared validity, an explicit acceptance with its written confirmation, promised resources and arrival day, an arrival compared against the promise, and a release closing the commitment. **Exceeds:** the promise is compared line by line with exact shortfalls and late days; the withdrawal-then-release order is derived and printed; the breaches are kept and printed with exact days rather than repaired; the ask goes to the joint apex and never to the partner. No vendor environment was run and no hands-on parity is claimed.
