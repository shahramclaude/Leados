# ADR-0261 — Performance Calibration Decision Register

**Date:** 2026-11-18 · **Status:** Accepted for canonical source · **Surface:** `#/performance-calibration` · **Storage:** v212 → v213.

## Decision

A director or VP running a performance-calibration session should not have to trust a headline rating. Reviews collect a manager's proposed rating; stewardship and assessment-reconciliation flag that two lines proposed ratings two or more steps apart for the same person, but the record then stops — no committee session, no proposed rating, no calibrated final rating, no written rationale, and compensation (`comp.js`) pays directly off the **uncalibrated** `manager.rating`. This register closes that gap: **the calibrated rating is DERIVED from a recorded decision with written rationale and never typed as a free fact, and two divergent proposals are NEVER averaged** — the committee declares one, or the divergence stays printed. Primary altitude **director / VP with mid-manager execution** × **single-chain and matrix** (a person with two managers has two proposed ratings from two lines, so every ask resolves the joint apex above ALL of them). The real duty: a hand records a calibration session, the committee records each decision with its rationale, and the register holds the organisation to the declared distribution and the written basis for every adjustment.

## Evidence and calculation

- **The calibrated rating is a recorded decision, not a stored state.** A calibration session binds a cycle label, a scope, a target distribution (counts per published rating key) and 1–4 responsible hands. A decision binds (session, person, review) with the proposed rating (re-read from the review, never typed), the calibrated rating (a closed rating key), a written rationale and the deciding hand. The reader re-derives every state on read: `uncalibrated` (no decision), `calibrated` (calibrated = proposed), `adjusted` (calibrated ≠ proposed, rationale printed beside both), `divergent-uncalibrated` (two or more lines proposed ratings two or more steps apart with no calibrated rating reconciling them), and `overdue-session` (session open past the declared warn horizon with uncalibrated persons). **Nothing derived is stored** — a session recorded as balanced whose decisions say otherwise prints the divergence, because the reader recomputes it from its inputs.
- **The distributions are printed, never averaged.** Proposed and calibrated distributions are counts per published rating key (`needs-development` `meets-expectations` `often-exceeds` `sets-standard`). The proposed rating is read from the review and must match it at the door (a decision whose proposed rating disagrees with the review is refused); the calibrated rating is a closed key. Two divergent proposals are never averaged into a calibrated rating. `figuresComplete` is separate from `balances` — the reader's totals re-add to its rows, and an unknown rating is counted, not invented.
- **Matrix correctness (ADR-0140/0141).** A person with two managers has two proposed ratings from two active lines. The reader resolves every ACTIVE line holding the person through the ONE matrix engine, counts a person ONCE, and derives the joint apex over every line as the seat whose authority answers for the calibration. Where a hand is off the graph the row is printed as an orphan rather than dropped, because deleting it would erase a live calibration duty from the record.
- **The bias screen is a heuristic, not a conclusion.** The four-fifths ratio (EEOC 1978) is computed per declared level (calibrated where exists, else proposed): favourable = `often-exceeds` + `sets-standard`, ratio below 0.8 flags. Refused below the floor — a screening heuristic, never a legal finding, and never a score or rank of a person.
- **RULE-CAL-01** (spine #169, `calAutoPropagate`) files at most one dated ask per condition per joint seat, capped at 4 per commit with the remainder counted: `divergent-uncalibrated`, `overdue-session`, `distribution-flag` (four-fifths below 0.8, not refused), `uncalibrated-person` (open session, uncalibrated past the warn horizon). Every ask goes to the **lowest joint apex above every line holding a responsible hand** — never to one line on another's behalf — and where nobody can be asked the rule writes ONE identity-free `calMarkers` observation and substitutes nobody. Ask text is deliberately opaque (a calibration schedule names people and ratings, and a delegation is readable above the addressee); the `cal1:<kind>:<subjectId>` signature lives only in the typed `issues-obligation` link. Asks are self-closing when the condition clears and answered-never-refiled; pause-governed on every path, including the explicit scan.

## Boundaries and costs

- Three collections, all empty at upgrade (`calSessions`, `calDecisions`, `calMarkers`) + three bounded conventions (`calAutoPropagate` defaulted ON, `calPrivacyFloor` 3, `calWarnHorizonDays` 14). **Nothing is inferred** from a review, a comp run or a person record: manufacturing a calibration decision at upgrade would put a committee act on the record no hand accepted. Loaders are fail-closed (a row that cannot be read exactly is dropped with one counted aggregate warning; a decision naming no parent session or no review is dropped; an off-roster hand is KEPT and printed as an orphan — deleting it would erase a live duty); the door refuses every write the loader would refuse on reload — a decision with no rationale, a proposed rating that disagrees with the review, a calibrated rating outside the closed set, and a write to a closed session are all refused at the door.
- Read/write reach is recomputed on the live reporting graph (`read-performance-calibration` / `manage-performance-calibration`). Above rank 4 the reader prints counts and withholds person detail; the CSV (#197 `performance-calibration`) is aggregate, identity-free and floored — counts and published-vocabulary keys only, with a `blank_reason` where the floor withholds a per-state count. Seats are workflow declarations in one browser-owned workspace, not authentication. The register scores, ranks and rates **nothing and nobody**: a nine-box ranking or a person score is exactly the fake precision this build refuses, consistent with the `performance.js` honesty boundary.
- **Asset provenance (mandate):** Higgsfield is unavailable in this environment (S323–S331). This build is a data/governance register with no bespoke visual asset; the CSV export and on-screen panels carry no placeholder imagery. Real-browser/AT walkthrough **[UNVERIFIED]**.

## Benchmark and remainder

The specialist bar is the performance-calibration tooling Lattice, Culture Amp, 15Five, Betterworks and Workday set: a **distribution view**, a **live calibration session**, **lock-after-calibration** with an **audit log**, **bias alerts** and **comp integration** [1](https://www.confirm.com/blog/best-performance-calibration-software-2026-top-8-tools) [2](https://hrtechsaas.com/blog/best-performance-calibration-software/). What those platforms do not all do is make the **calibrated rating a derived, rationale-required record** and refuse to **average divergent proposals**: they let a facilitator drag a distribution and lock it, but the adjustment rationale and the matrix joint-apex duty are not the spine of the record. This register's bar is the opposite discipline — the calibrated rating is a decision with written rationale, divergence is printed rather than averaged, and every ask resolves the joint apex above all lines. Primary-vendor same-input trials are **[BENCHMARK-UNVERIFIED]** (this sandbox reached secondary comparison roundups only; no live Lattice/Culture Amp workspace was reachable).

**In Progress / unbuilt (logged in ROADMAP.md):** an automated comp-link that pays only off a calibrated rating once a session is closed; a multi-cycle calibration trend view; a bias-alert escalation beyond the four-fifths screen. None of this register is HR advice, a legal finding or a claim that any rating is fair; it is the record that lets a director see what a calibration session decided, why, and what it still owes.

## Post-hoc repair note (ADR-0262 session, 2026-11-18)

This register shipped with four defects the ADR-0262 session's defect hunt found and repaired; they are recorded here rather than left silent:

1. **The seat gate did not fail closed.** `calActions` discarded the `canAct` verdict — a truthy `{ allow: false, … }` object PASSED the `Boolean()` check — and fell back to `seats[0]`, so a refused or wrong-seated caller was treated as seated. The gate is now the `msaActions` `seatFor` pattern verbatim: the seat is the one `activeSeat(built)` resolves, the right's verdict must be `allow: true`, and there is no first-seat fallback. Covered by a standing-repair test in `performance-calibration.test.mjs`.
2. **The wiring was never carried.** `read/manage-performance-calibration` were absent from the access gateway's right→command map (writes under a declared seat were refused as unknown API names), `calAutoPropagate` was absent from the pause-permit census maps (the census could not see the rule it silences), and `performance-calibration` was absent from the disclosure-compiler `RENDERER_ROUTES` census (route integrity was never proved). All three are registered.
3. **The view's tables rendered `—` in every cell.** `table()` implements object columns (`{ label, render | key }`); this register's view passed string columns with array rows, which the component contract does not accept. The tables now use the object contract — a displayed==computed defect, since the screen claimed to print reader figures it never printed.
4. **Nothing here is re-derived:** the repairs change only refusals, wiring and rendering. No stored row, derived state or reader figure was altered.
