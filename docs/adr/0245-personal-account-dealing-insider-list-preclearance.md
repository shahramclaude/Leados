# ADR-0245 — Personal-Account Dealing, Insider Lists & Closed-Period Pre-Clearance: who was on the list, when trading was closed, who cleared it, and what actually traded — printed, never repaired

- **Status:** accepted and built — Schema v199, route `#/personal-dealing`, `RULE-DWL-01` on the shared spine, CSV scope #186, demo seed, insights signals (DWL-01..05), focused suite 29/29.
- **Date:** 2026-11-17
- **Deciders:** A results announcement is five days away and dealing is closed for twenty-one days before it. A campaign has been running for six days with no decision. A trade was executed four days into that window, and whether it was approved is a question nobody can answer from the record — because the workspace can store a decision, a delegation and a conflict of interest, and **none of them can store an insider list, a closed period, a clearance request and the trade that followed it**. This register exists to answer four questions the workspace could not previously ask: **who is on the insider list and on what written basis, when is dealing closed for them, who independently cleared what, and what actually traded.**
- **Schema:** **v199** (v198 → v199). Six additive collections, all **empty at upgrade**: `dwlDesignations`, `dwlEvents`, `dwlClearances`, `dwlDecisions`, `dwlDealings`, `dwlMarkers`. No designation, closed period, clearance, decision, dealing or marker is inferred from anything an upgraded workspace already holds — a results announcement in a calendar, an existing delegation and a person's roster level all mention the same world, and none of them becomes a personal-dealing record here. Three bounded conventions: `dwlAutoPropagate` (pause switch, defaulted ON), `dwlPrivacyFloor` 3 [2–20], `dwlClearanceTurnaroundDays` 3 [1–30]. **There is deliberately no default window**: `beforeDays` and `afterDays` are declared per event and have no convention, because a closed period is a human declaration about a real event, never a system guess.
- **Surface:** `#/personal-dealing` — five panels: List (who is designated, on what basis, from when, on which lines, and whether they hold a seat at all), Windows (every declared closed period, its span, and the verdict for each listed person today), Clearances (the request, who asked, the decision, who decided and whether they were independent, the validity), Dealings (what traded, when, against which clearance, and every breach attached to it), Method (the framework basis, the state vocabulary, and what the register refuses to do). Seven dialogs: declare a designation, declare a closed period, request a clearance, decide a clearance, record a dealing, end a designation, conventions.

## Context

Personal-account dealing is the oldest control in market-abuse regulation and the one every workspace stores in a spreadsheet. The UK Market Abuse Regulation (retained MAR art. 19) requires PDMRs to notify; art. 18 requires an **insider list** kept up to date with the basis for each entry. The same shape appears in the US (Section 16, Rule 10b5-1 plans, Rule 144) and under the EU MAR. **Given the user's location in Denmark, Finanstilsynet supervision under EU MAR art. 18/19 is the governing frame.** The vendor bar is set by the employee-trade-compliance tools — StarCompliance, ComplySci, MyComplianceOffice **[BENCHMARK-VERIFIED against fetched vendor pages]** — and their common core is exactly this register: pre-trade clearance with an explicit decision, per-individual restriction scoping, watch/restricted/**blackout** lists, holding periods, broker statement feeds, attestations, and audit-ready reporting.

The practitioner failure modes are record failures, not app failures:

1. **The insider list is a name and a date.** Somebody was "on the list"; nobody can say on what written basis, from when, on which lines, or whether they are still on it. Under art. 18 the basis is the point.
2. **The closed period is common knowledge and nowhere written.** Everybody knows not to trade before results. Nobody can say on which day the window opened, on which day it closes, or that the answer today is `closed`.
3. **A request stands in for an approval.** Somebody asked. Somebody said yes in a corridor. Silence is read as permission — the single most dangerous inference in the whole control.
4. **The person who asked approves their own request.** A self-approval is not an approval; it is the exact thing the control exists to prevent.
5. **The approver cannot see what they approved.** A disclosure rule that hands the decider a count instead of the security does not protect the data — it disables the control.
6. **A trade is refused instead of recorded.** A control that refuses to record a bad trade destroys its own evidence. The trade happened; the register's job is to keep it and say what it was.
7. **The breach is repaired away.** A trade inside a closed window "must have been" cleared; an approval dated before its request "must have been" a typo. The register carries what the record says and prints the arithmetic.

## Decision

Build the register as one reader over six append-only ledgers, with the states **derived on every read** and **never stored**.

**1. The four objects, and nothing else.** A *designation* is a person on the insider list, with a closed-vocabulary basis and a **written** instrument reference (a clause, a letter, a list version — not "insider" as a mood). An *event* is a declared closed period: a kind, a subject, the day it discloses, and the whole-day bounds before and after it. A *clearance* is a request to deal, naming the person, the security, the side and the quantity. A *decision* is a clearance's verdict, its written reason where refused, and its validity in whole days. A *dealing* is what actually traded, with the broker's contract note as its proof. The loaders are fail-closed: anything that cannot be read exactly is **dropped with a counted warning and never repaired**.

**2. A designation must name its written basis.** `instrumentRef` is mandatory and has a real minimum length, because art. 18 is about the *basis*, not the name. A designation whose end runs backwards is dropped.

**3. No default window, ever.** `beforeDays` and `afterDays` are declared per event and have no convention and no fallback. An event whose bounds cannot be resolved produces `dealt-window-undeterminable` — **never** a silent pass. `windowSpan` computes the span once and every consumer reads it; the arithmetic is exact whole days with the ends inclusive, measured from the nearest edge, so `0` means "inside, on the edge" and `null` means "outside". Those two must never be confused.

**4. Silence is not an approval.** A clearance with no decision reads `pending` until its declared turnaround passes and then `pending-overdue` with the exact days late. There is no state in which an unanswered request counts as cleared.

**5. A person can never decide their own clearance — and the DOOR refuses to create one.** `dwlDecideClearance` refuses `personal-dealing:self-decision` when the deciding seat is the requesting person. A decision already in the record that names the requester as decider is **kept and printed** as `breach-decided-by-requester` — the loader never deletes evidence, and the warning names the fact.

**6. Independence is proved, not named.** The decider must be **strictly above the requester on every line that holds them**, computed through `matrixJointApex` against the requester *and every one of their line managers*. In the fixture, mei is sam's home manager and freya holds a project line: neither mei nor freya alone is independent (freya is not above mei, so she cannot act on mei's request), and priya — the one seat above both lines — is. A rank-2 manager who is not above the requester is refused by name, not by rank.

**7. Detail follows the duty.** This is the rule that had to be got right. The obvious design — "above rank 2, counts only" — **is wrong on its own**, and the way it is wrong matters: the seat that must decide a clearance is by construction strictly above the requester, usually a director or a VP, and a decider who cannot see the security and the quantity is being asked to approve something they are forbidden to look at. A disclosure rule that blinds the decider does not protect the data, it **disables the control**. So a row is shown in detail to a seat that (a) has full access, (b) sits at rank 2 or below, (c) **holds** the person through a span or declared line, (d) **is** the person, or (e) is a seat the graph proves above them. Every other high-altitude seat is handed counts and published state keys, and the page **prints which rows are withheld and why**. This is a rendering contract, not an authentication boundary — the workspace has no authentication and implying otherwise would be the lie that hurts people.

**8. A listed person with no operating seat is a printed limitation.** An operating seat exists only for a leadership node. A designated individual contributor with no reports and no line of their own **holds no seat**, so the gateway refuses every write from them — including their own clearance request. The register therefore prints `holdsSeat: false` per person and a counted `listedWithoutSeatN`, and the record keeps `requestedById` beside `personId` so a record with no self-request is read as *that limitation*, never as evidence the person acted. This is the honest handling; inventing a seat would be inventing a fact about the organisation.

**9. A bad trade is recorded, not refused.** `dwlRecordDealing` accepts a trade with no clearance, a trade inside a closed window and a trade after a refusal. The states are the evidence: `dealt-without-clearance`, `breach-dealt-in-closed-window`, `breach-dealt-after-refusal`, `breach-dealt-outside-clearance`, `dealt-before-designation`, `dealt-after-designation-ended`, `dealt-window-undeterminable`. The window is the graver fact, so a cleared trade inside a closed window reads `breach-dealt-in-closed-window` — while the clearance it cited still reads `cleared-and-dealt`. **Both facts are true and both are printed**; `breaches` is always a list, never collapsed to the gravest one.

**10. `RULE-DWL-01` is spine rule 158/158 (one rule, one contract).** Four conditions: `dealing-breach`, `clearance-overdue`, `window-open`, `clearance-requested`. Each is filed at most once per `(dwl1:… signature, addressee)`, bounded at 4 asks per commit with the remainder counted, asks the **joint apex above every line holding the person** and **never the person**, and is **opaque** in its text — no person, no security, no broker reference, no quantity, no day — because a delegation is readable above the addressee and the typed `issues-obligation` link carries the identity instead. A seat that cannot be resolved writes **one identity-free `dwlMarkers` observation** per condition and substitutes nobody. Every path, including the explicit scan, is pause-governed on `dwlAutoPropagate` — because a closed period **opens and closes by the calendar** and a turnaround runs out with nobody having written anything, the rule reads the whole workspace on every commit.

**11. The CSV boundary is aggregate and identity-free.** #186 exports one summary row and one row per printed dealing state and clearance state — counts only. No person, no basis per person, no security reference, no broker reference, no quantity and no day. A per-state count below the declared privacy floor is exported **BLANK with its reason**, never as `0` (which would be a different, false claim) and never as the number (which would defeat the floor). `complete` is kept apart from the counts.

## Consequences

- The workspace can now answer, from its own record: who is on the insider list and on what written basis; whether dealing is closed for a given person today, and by how many days; who asked for clearance, who decided it and whether that decider was independent; what actually traded against what was approved; and exactly which breaches exist, each with its arithmetic.
- The demo record exercises the whole vocabulary against the injected clock — a window **closed today**, a trade **four days inside it**, an overdue decision, a written refusal nobody dealt against, and a trade with no clearance at all — so nothing in the demo is a state that was only true on the day it was written.
- **Refusals are the product.** Nine named refusals stand between the UI and a record: `self-decision`, `not-above-requester`, `reason-missing`, `validity-missing`, `instrument-missing`, `basis-unknown`, `quantity-invalid`, `day-invalid`, `clearance-not-found`. Each names the fact rather than silently declining.
- **The self-approval path is deliberately left open at the loader and closed at the door.** A workspace that imports a self-decided clearance keeps it, sees it, and reads it as a breach. Deleting it would destroy the evidence the control exists to produce. This is a recorded decision, flagged here so no later session "fixes" it.
- **Honest limitation, carried in the product:** a designated individual contributor holds no operating seat, so the hand that holds them records on their behalf. The screen says so in words, and the record prints who asked beside who it was for.
- **Not built, and deliberately:** broker statement feeds (the contract note is recorded, never ingested), market-price feeds, GICS/sector scoping of restrictions, 10b5-1 plan lifecycle, and attestation campaigns. Each is a real capability in the benchmarked vendors and each is named as a gap rather than implied by a menu item.

**Exceeded, explicitly:** exact whole-day arithmetic printed beside every verdict (days inside the window, days late, validity close); joint-apex ask routing that never asks the person whose dealing is the subject; per-row disclosure that keeps the decider sighted; and an identity-free export that blanks a below-floor count with a reason instead of printing `0`.

## Gaps named by this session

1. **Broker and custodian statement ingestion** — the highest-value missing integration. Every benchmarked vendor has it; the controlling record is only as complete as what is recorded.
2. **Watch list and restricted list** as distinct objects from a closed period — a named-security restriction with its own reason and end, scoped by person, line or role.
3. **10b5-1 / pre-declared plan lifecycle** — a standing instruction that displaces window-by-window clearance, with its own cooling-off and certification requirements.
4. **Attestation campaigns** — the annual code-of-ethics return and its overdue state.
5. **Sector/GICS scoping** of a restriction to a security class rather than a person.

All five are logged in `ROADMAP.md` as unbuilt.
